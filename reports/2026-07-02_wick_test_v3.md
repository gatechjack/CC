## The fixes did what they were designed to — and it still fails
- **Selective entry (DR) works:** fill rate **66–72%** (vs v2's vacuous 100%), ~140–175 fills/wk. Selection

- **Wider stop works:** R-unit now **0.16–0.27%** of price (1.5–2.7× v2's 0.1%); body-close realized losses are

- **Yet 0 cells clear the null.** Fixing the mechanics did not create edge, because the edge was never there.

## Why it fails (three converging pieces of evidence)

### 1. The short "edge" is bear-beta — and *worse* than random shorts
Windows are strongly bear: drift BTC −4.5%, ETH −33.4%, SOL −24.4%, XRP −29.3%. Every positive cell is a
**short**, and every one **fails its direction-matched null** — which is *elevated* precisely because a random
short with the same k·ATR geometry profits from the drift:
| cell | BODY avgR | null p95 | beats? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ETH short 2R none | +0.243 | **+0.290** | no |
| ETH short 2R disp | +0.289 | **+0.329** | no |
| BTC short 2R none | +0.110 | **+0.120** | no |
| SOL short 1.0 none | +0.034 | **+0.170** | no |
| XRP short 2R none | +0.099 | **+0.252** | no |
The wick-test shorts earn *less* than randomly shorting the same bear window with the same stop/target. The
drift-embedding null does exactly its job: **the setup adds negative value over passive bear-beta.** (Passive-
short whole-window ≈ +28R BTC / +156R ETH / +91R SOL / +128R XRP in 1.5-ATR units — the bear is the "edge.")

### 2. Longs are negative almost everywhere
Best long is BTC atr1.5 disp at **+0.024R** (fails null +0.097); most longs −0.04…−0.16R. No long cell is
meaningfully positive → the "both sides" gate fails hard (no long *or* short passes). A real setup edge would
show on both sides; this shows on neither once drift is controlled.

### 3. ★ DR-skip diagnostic — the entry mechanic filters out the WINNERS
Triggered-but-unfilled setups (price broke and held above L but **never came back for the retest**) would have
been **big winners** if entered at bar-3 close:
| k | skips | would-have-been avgR (body, 2R) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 4553 | **+0.760** |
| 1.5 | 4553 | **+0.585** |
| 2.0 | 4553 | **+0.447** |
Positive on **every** coin×side (+0.45…+0.70R). The setups that *ran* (no retest) were the strong continuations;
the DR retest requirement **systematically keeps the choppy ones that come back to chop you out and discards the
runners.** This is v1's fill-selection problem, confirmed and quantified: **the "honest limit / retest-into-the-
wick" entry is anti-selective for a momentum-continuation setup.** (Caveat: the +0.58R is *forward-conditioned*
on "didn't retest" — you can't know that at bar-3 close — so it is **not a tradeable alternative**, only proof
that the retest discipline removes the winners. v2 tried entering every break at market and was gross-negative,
so chasing all breaks isn't the answer either.)

## HARD vs BODY under the wider stop — body-close's value shrinks
Shakeout survival still nets positive per cell (+12…+456R) but is now only **2–13% of trades** (vs v2's 12–28%):
the wider stop already tolerates the wicks that body-close used to rescue, so **their benefit overlaps** and
body-close's marginal value diminishes as the stop widens. HARD is now comparable to or better than BODY in many
ATR cells (e.g., BTC short 1.5: hard +0.101 vs body +0.068). v2's finding (body-close > hard) was largely an
artifact of the too-tight stop; widen the stop and the gap closes.

## Ledger
**Wick test retired — three independent strikes.** The mechanization is honest and instrumented; the setup has
no tradeable gross edge on BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP over this 47–81d (bear-dominant) window. The deepest, reusable
learning: **a retest-into-the-wick entry is structurally anti-selective for momentum continuation** (it discards
the runners), and **short "edges" in a bear window must be gated against a drift-embedding direction-matched
null** or they read as alpha when they are beta. Not recommending further wick-test variants; if momentum-
continuation is revisited, the entry problem (capture runners without forward-conditioning) is the thing to
solve first, on a multi-regime window.

## Caveats
47–81d one-bear-regime (dominates the short cells); GROSS only; DR-skip would-have-been is forward-conditioned
(diagnostic, not tradeable); literal-stop cells are reference (not null-gated, not in the gate). No lookahead:
impulse/level/confirm/ATR/regime read from closed bars; DR fill and sims strictly forward, stop-first.
