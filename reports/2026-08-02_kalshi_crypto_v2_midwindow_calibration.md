# S4 Mid-Window Calibration — overreaction-fade evidence base

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Scope:** Kalshi 15m up/down binaries, BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP, FULL settled corpus (not just holdout).  
**Standing:** read-only; on-disk (no pulls); lab DB only; evidence only — no verdict.

Contract-implied P(YES) at minute m = the traded price (`price_mean`) of the minute-m candle. Realized y = S1 settlement (RTI 60s-avg vs open floor_strike). **Calibration gap = realized_YES_freq − mean(implied_p)**; gap<0 where implied is high ⇒ market OVERSTATES that side (overreaction that fades). Minutes 1-13 (open tick + settlement-convergence minute excluded). Move = signed underlying %move (Binance 1m) open→minute m. Directional windows only (|settlement move|≥0.05%) unless noted; t = gap/SE.

## Reading this (evidence, not verdict)

- **Overreaction-fade** shows as: at the extreme move buckets, gap<0 on big UP moves (implied overstated YES, realized fades) AND gap>0 on big DOWN moves (implied overstated NO). A symmetric, time-decaying gap on the tails is the fade signal; a ~0 gap everywhere means the mid-window price is calibrated (no fade to harvest).
- **|t|<~2 ⇒ indistinguishable from calibrated.** Gaps are per observation (windows contribute multiple correlated minutes), so treat SEs as OPTIMISTIC (serial correlation not adjusted) — a follow-up would cluster by window.
- Flat windows (settlement move <0.05%) are ~coin-flip by construction; the directional split isolates the real moves. Regime conditions whether any fade is trend- or range-specific.
