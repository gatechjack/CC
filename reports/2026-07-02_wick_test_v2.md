## VERDICT (short)
**NO build. v2 is gross-negative everywhere** (worse than v1). Of 96 cells, exactly **one** clears its null —
XRP long 2R (mom, body): avgR **+0.007R** — and only because the **body-close null is itself negative** (−0.052);
a ~0R cell beating a negative bar is not an edge. **But the central hypothesis half-holds:** the **body-close
discipline is the single most valuable element** — shakeout survivors are **12–28% of trades** and body-close
beats hard-stop in nearly every cell (often by +200…+1600R total), pulling the best cells from badly negative to
**near-breakeven**. It **rescues but does not reach +EV**: the deeper realized losses (median −1.4…−1.6R, tails to
−25R) offset the shakeout gains. The **local momentum filter does not separate.** With the constant 0.1% R-unit,
even break-even gross is net-negative after fees.

## Two structural findings (flag first — they define v2)
1. **Fill rate = 100% (triggered == filled) — the "retest tap" is vacuous by construction.** Entry bar *k*
   opens at the prior close (≤ L) and then breaks above L, so its low is *necessarily* ≤ L → the limit at L
   always fills on the trigger bar. So v2 is really **"enter at the 2-bar impulse high on every break"**:
   **~415–475 fills/week** per coin·side (vs v1's 30–60/wk at 58–68% fill). The pre-positioned limit adds no
   retest selection. `bar-4-would-have-fill = 0` everywhere (nothing to defer).
2. **Same-bar stop+fill ~25–40% of fills** (BTC ~21%, ETH/SOL/XRP ~33–47% of *fills*; counts 970–1,315). Entry
   = L, stop 0.1% below L, but the entry bar's own range routinely exceeds 0.1%, so the bar that fills also wicks
   through the stop → stop-first → **immediate −1R under HARD**. **The 0.1% stop is smaller than the entry bar's
   own range** — the dominant loss source. (Under BODY-CLOSE these often survive — see below.)

## Null note — why "beats null" is a weak bar under body-close
Because body-close losses are fat-tailed, the **random-entry body-close null has a NEGATIVE p95** (e.g., −0.052).
So a cell can "beat the null" while still losing money — the single beat (XRP long 2R mom body, +0.007R) is
exactly that: ~break-even gross clearing a negative random baseline. **Not evidence of edge.** Under HARD (null
p95 mildly positive, as in v1) **no cell beats null at all.**

## Answers to the three verdict questions
1. **Does pre-positioned-limit + body-close clear the null where v1 didn't?** **No.** v2 is a *stronger* gross
   loser than v1; the lone null-beat is a break-even cell over a negative body-close null (meaningless). The
   vacuous 100%-fill entry + 0.1% stop is dominated by same-bar shakeouts.
2. **Is body-close the edge-carrier (shakeout survival paying for deeper losses)?** **Directionally yes — this is
   the real finding.** Shakeout survival is large (12–28%) and body-close beats hard everywhere; but the −1.4…−25R
   loss tail means it only pays *back most of* the loss, reaching break-even at best, not profit.
3. **Does the LOCAL momentum filter separate?** **No.**

## Recommendation & the one forward idea
**NO build** — gross-negative, fee-dominated (constant 0.1% R-unit). The demonstrated lever is **body-close
tolerance of shakeouts**, not this entry. A *new spike* (not a tweak) worth considering: pair body-close with a
**more selective entry** (v1's retest-fill did real filtering that v2's vacuous fill discards) and a **wider stop**
so the R-unit isn't dominated by same-bar noise + fees — then re-test whether shakeout survival can carry a
setup into genuine +EV. As-specified, v2 does not.

## Caveats
47–81d one-bear-regime; GROSS only (fees would deepen every cell); constant-0.1% R-unit; body-close loss-first
resolution is conservative (tp-would-fill-first ~1–2%, immaterial); one-open-at-a-time gated by HARD exit so
HARD/BODY share identical fills (body-close may overlap the next fill — mild correlation). No lookahead:
impulse/level/momentum/regime all read from closed bars; fill and sim strictly intrabar-forward with stop-first.
