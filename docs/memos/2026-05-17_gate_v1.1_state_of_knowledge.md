## 1. Original problem statement

The BitUnix Futures division (3m BTC scalping, paper-only) runs a price-
action validator gate that sits between the score engine and order
placement. The gate has 3 validators (vwap_alignment, volume_confirmation,
structure_alignment) with `require_all=true` semantics. Across a recent
9.5h prod observation window:

- 205 PA rejects on STANDARD + PREMIUM tier score-wins → **100% reject rate**
- 11 deferred-fire waits expired without redeeming; 0 redeemed
- 0/11 expired waits crossed a 4h UTC structure boundary (the
  `structure_alignment` validator was mechanically frozen across each
  wait window because the 4h validator compares only completed 4h
  buckets and no boundary crossed during any wait)

Two archived memos document this (`docs/memos/2026-05-18_pa_structure_tf_change.md`
and the matching `_backtest_results.md`). Both predate this work.

The PA gate is calibrated for hostile regimes — Board chart-reviewed
individual rejections and judged them correct (per
`feedback_pa_gate_well_calibrated.md`). But the gate also misses
opportunities the structure check shouldn't have to weigh in on, and the
mechanism is fundamentally tied to a timeframe (4h) that's 80× the
trade horizon (3m). The Board commissioned a redesign.

## 2. What we built

### v1.0 — initial 5-factor confluence gate

Replaced PA validator's 3-of-3 require-all with a 5-factor gate that
counts confirmations across timeframes:

| Factor | TF | Pass condition (long) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. EMA alignment | 15m | EMA8 > EMA21 > EMA50 AND EMA8 slope > 0 |
| 2. VWAP | 3m + prior-day | price > session VWAP AND price > prior-day VWAP |
| 3. Volatility | 5m | ATR(14) > SMA(ATR, 50) AND BB pct-rank ≥ 10% |
| 4. CVD | 15m window from 3m | slope > 0 (tick-rule fallback) |
| 5. Volume z-score | 3m × 20-bar | z ≥ 1.0 |

Sell-side mirrors with inequalities flipped. Fires when score ≥ 3.
Pre-committed Board-locked acceptance bar (mod #1): profit factor ≥ 1.20,
win rate ≥ 45%, ≥20 round-trips, fire rate ∈ [5%, 50%] of alerts.

v1.0 backtest result (17d window, 2026-04-30 → 2026-05-17, 1,796 prod
alerts vs Coinbase 1m OHLCV): **PF=2.01, WR=48.5%, n=33 trades, fire
rate=1.84%.** PASS on 4/5 acceptance checks; FAIL on fire rate (1.84% <
5% floor). Full report: `reports/gate_backtest_2026-05-17.md`.

### v1.1 — two defects identified and fixed

Mid-investigation surfacing in `reports/gate_backtest_2026-05-17_factor_analysis.md`
§ Q5 (code audit of side-conditional logic) found two issues:

1. **CVD semantic bug.** `cvd_from_bars_tick_rule` computed
   `linregress_slope(per_bar_deltas)`, not slope of the cumulative
   CVD series. In sustained-trend tapes (most relevant scalping
   regimes), per-bar deltas form a flat-ish line → slope ≈ 0 → factor
   returned False for BOTH sides regardless of side. The cumulative
   formulation produces a slope that correctly reflects net flow
   direction. v1.1 fixes this AND switches the sign source from
   `close - prev_close` (inter-bar momentum) to `close - open`
   (intra-bar candle direction) per Board spec.
2. **F1 spec deviation.** EMA factor only checked `slope(ema_8)`,
   not all three slopes. Phase A test fixtures encoded the v1.0
   single-slope behavior; the dataclass `GateInputs` didn't even
   have slots for ema_21/ema_50 slopes. v1.1 adds the missing slope
   fields and requires all three aligned with side.

Five tests added (TDD before fixes, all initially failing on v1.0
code). Fixture updates pushed to four existing tests that encoded
v1.0 behavior. Post-mortem documents why Phase A's tests didn't catch
either defect — see `reports/gate_backtest_2026-05-17_v2.md` § post-mortem.

v1.1 backtest result (same 17d window): **PF=2.63, WR=54.8%, n=31
trades, fire rate=1.73%.** PASS on 4/5; still FAIL on fire rate.
Headline metric improvements vs v1.0: PF +0.62, WR +6.3pp, total R
+5.07. CVD pass rate dropped 6.3pp as expected; EMA pass rate barely
moved (EMAs are smooth; all-three-slopes is mostly redundant with
ema_8 slope in practice).

## 3. What the 17-day backtest tells us — and what it doesn't

### What it tells us

- v1.1 5-factor gate is **selective and profitable** on the 17-day
  benign-ish window: WR ≈ 55%, PF ≈ 2.6, total R ≈ +21 across 31
  trades.
- v1.1 beats v1.0 on every quality metric (WR, Avg R, Total R, PF)
  after the two correctness fixes.
- v1.1 beats the PA gate on total R (+21.23 vs +14.92) but with
  more selectivity — fire rate stayed below the 5% pre-committed
  floor in both versions.
- Factor-contribution analysis (`_factor_analysis.md` § Q2) found:
  the gate rejects **24 PA-only trades** (PA fired, 5f didn't) that
  collectively were net-positive in the PA arm (+16.92R, 54% WR).
  The 5f gate cost ~+16R that PA captured — entirely on rejects
  driven by ema_alignment (20/24) + volume_z (19/24).
- Code audit found NO asymmetric side-conditional bug. The gate's
  directional behaviour is market-following (a sell-tape makes
  sell-side factor conditions easier to satisfy; the gate then fires
  more sells). Not a bug.

### What it doesn't tell us

- **Whether v1.1 generalizes to other regimes.** The 17-day window
  was a sell-dominant tape (82% sell-intent alerts). Behavior in a
  buy-tape or chop regime is unknown.
- **Whether the small sample (31 trades) is statistically significant.**
  PF 2.6 with n=31 has wide uncertainty bars.
- **What happens during regime transitions.** The gate has no regime
  classifier; it just sums confirmations.

## 4. What the hybrid Bybit-bar backtest adds

The hybrid backtest re-runs v1.1 on the same 17-day prod-alert window
but swaps Coinbase 1m OHLCV for Bybit 3m/15m (from `data/btc_scalping.db`)
plus Bitunix 5m (from cached pull). This isolates the **venue-fidelity
correction** — what changes when the gate is fed bars from a venue
closer to the actual BitUnix trading venue?

**Headline result (full report: `reports/gate_backtest_2026-05-17_v3_bybit_hybrid.md`):**

| Metric | v1.1 / Coinbase | v1.1 / Bybit-hybrid | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fires | 31 | 32 | +1 |
| Win rate | 54.8% | **31.2%** | **−23.6 pp** |
| Profit factor | 2.63 | **1.14** | **−1.49** |
| Avg R | +0.685 | +0.080 | −0.605 |
| Total R | +21.23 | +2.55 | −18.68 |
| Fire rate | 1.73% | 2.45% | +0.72 pp |

**3 of 4 Phase C pre-committed acceptance thresholds fail on Bybit-
hybrid bars** (PF<1.20, WR<45%, fire-rate<5% floor; only n≥20 clears).

### What Block B isolates

Per-factor pass rates are stable across the synth-17d and synth-31d
windows (max delta +3.6pp on vwap; all others within ±2pp; ±5pp
diagnostic flag does NOT fire). v1.1's factor mechanics behave
consistently across input distributions.

Critically: **synth-17d WR=31.1% matches prod-17d WR=31.2% exactly**
on the same Bybit bars. Same gate, same bar source, two completely
different alert sources, identical outcome. This isolates the
verdict-collapse to **bars + trade-resolution**, NOT alert-source
or factor instability.

### Why the verdict collapsed (working hypothesis)

Two non-exclusive explanations, both testable:

1. **Bar-resolution dominant.** Coinbase 1m gave the v1.1 baseline
   finer trade-resolution; Bybit 3m (no 1m source available in this
   run) puts SL/TP arbitration at coarser granularity where the
   SL-first-when-same-bar rule fires more often. Testable: pull
   Bybit 1m, re-run, see if WR recovers.
2. **Bybit alert-time prices differ from Coinbase.** TradingView
   alerts fire on TV's internal price feed; matching them to a
   different bar source produces different effective entry prices
   for the same alert. The price delta compounds through SL/TP
   placement.

### The CVD-fallback caveat

100% of evaluations used the OHLCV-proxy tick-rule fallback (Bybit
DB has no taker-volume columns). Real CVD on Bybit might shift
score distribution materially. CVD pass rate barely moved between
runs (56.7% Coinbase → 55.7% Bybit), but the underlying signal is a
proxy, not real CVD.

### What this changes about § 8's decision

The "paper cutover with shadow-PA" decision still stands, but the
framing is now sharper: **paper data must discriminate between two
competing explanations of v1.1's Bybit-hybrid verdict-collapse.**
- If paper PF reverts to ≥1.20 → backtest was bar-fidelity-limited.
- If paper PF stays near 1.14 or below → v1.1 was over-fit to v1.0's
  Coinbase-1m artifacts and needs structural rework.

Both possibilities should be named in the cutover decision memo so
the 60-day shadow data is read on the correct prior.

## 5. Documented limitations

### 5a. Coinbase-vs-Bybit venue fidelity gap (v1.0/v1.1 baseline)

The v1.0 and v1.1 backtests used Coinbase BTC/USD spot 1m OHLCV. Prod
trades on BitUnix BTCUSDT futures. Coinbase spot and BitUnix perp track
the same underlying asset but micro-venue differences exist.

**The 17d Bybit-hybrid backtest (§ 4) quantified the gap and found it
load-bearing:** PF 2.63 → 1.14, WR 54.8% → 31.2% on the same gate, same
alerts, swapped bar source. The Coinbase v1.1 result was the only PF-
clearing result; Block A showed it was bar-source-dependent.

### 5b. CVD tick-rule fallback (100% usage)

BitUnix's public feed has no aggressor-side trade flag. v1.1's CVD
factor uses a tick-rule fallback: `sign(close - open) * volume`,
cumulated, then linregressed. This is materially coarser than true
CVD. A future WebSocket trade-stream consumer would flip the
`cvd_fallback_used` flag to False and improve Factor 4's signal-to-
noise specifically. Out of scope for v1.

### 5c. Synth-alerts alertcondition gap (May 16 discovery)

`scripts/research_scoring/` infrastructure from May 16 synthesizes
alerts by scanning DB indicator columns for rising-edge transitions.
This produces **~91% more alerts than prod webhooks** on the same
17d window because:

- Multi-TF over-emission (synth fires one alert per (signal, tf) for
  3m + 15m + 30m; prod TV indicators are configured for one TF each)
- Cypher A/B columns fire 3-4× more than prod alertconditions
  (likely additional gating logic in the Pine Script that synth
  doesn't replicate)
- `pink_box_*` signals are present in prod (~15% of traffic) but
  absent from synth's DB column source

**Future contributors:** consult `reports/scoring_backtest_results.md`
lines 15–25 before attempting absolute-generalization claims on synth
alerts. The May 16 work explicitly accepted this limitation and
constrained itself to relative variant ranking. Track 2 (deferred —
see § 9) would inherit the same constraint.

### 5d. IS-contamination caveat for 31d backtest interpretation

The 31d truly-independent window (2026-03-30 → 2026-04-30) sits
**inside** the May 16 score-engine research's IS window (2026-03-30 →
2026-05-02). The May 16 variant recommendations (H2, H6b, H4b) were
selected partly based on performance over that data.

**But:** the May 16 recommendations were **not shipped** to prod
(per `reports/scoring_recommendation.md`). The scoring weights v1.1
sits on top of are pre-May-16 PR 3c baseline. So the contamination
affects hypothesis-selection bias for the score engine, NOT the
actual gate evaluation. Treat the 31d window as OOS for v1.1.

This caveat would matter if we ever ship the May 16 weight changes —
at that point the 31d backtest's relevance for retesting score-engine
changes would be diminished. For v1.1 gate validation: not load-bearing.

## 7. Public-domain context

The confluence-gate architecture (count confirmations across factors,
fire when score crosses threshold) is a **mainstream retail / scalping
pattern.** Public literature documents its strengths and known limits:

**Strengths it shares with the published-pattern lineage:**
- Robust to single-factor noise (one stale indicator doesn't gate out
  trades)
- Easy to add factors as new signal sources arrive
- Auditable: every fire decomposes into per-factor verdicts

**Known limitation the literature consistently flags:** confluence
gates **do not condition on regime**. They evaluate the same factor
thresholds in a trending market as in a chopping one, and in a high-
vol regime as in a calm one. The published failure mode: gates
calibrated on one regime under-fire or over-fire on different regimes.
Common architectural next step: **regime-classifier layer** sitting
above the confluence gate, modulating per-factor weights or thresholds
based on detected regime (trend / range / chop).

**v1.1 does not have a regime-classifier layer.** The HTF regime
gate that runs downstream of the confluence gate is a separate
permission-multiplier on the order, NOT a per-factor weight adjustment.
The 5-factor gate's evaluation is identical regardless of regime.

If paper data shows v1.1 over-fires (or under-fires) systematically in
specific regimes the 17d window didn't include, that's the architectural
prompt: **add a regime-classifier layer**. v1.1 is a v1 architecture and
may need this next step.

## 8. Decision space this memo supports

**Proposed cutover:** Paper-trading deployment of v1.1 with shadow-PA
logging for 60 days. Both gates evaluate every alert; 5f drives placement;
PA decision logged informationally for comparison.

**Why this works as a decision now:**
- v1.1 is materially better than v1.0 (correctness fixes shipped)
- v1.1 outperforms PA on 17-day Coinbase-bar window (PF, WR, total R)
- Backtest cannot answer the generalization question (prod data gap +
  Bybit-hybrid verdict-collapse — see § 4)
- Paper exposure is the only available path forward
- Shadow-PA logging preserves apples-to-apples comparison for 60 days

**Why it's not a slam-dunk decision (UPDATED 2026-05-18):**
- Pre-committed fire-rate floor (5%) NOT met on the 17d window
- 5f gate cost ~+16R of PA-only opportunity on 17d Coinbase window
- Sample size of 31 trades is small (statistical-significance issue)
- **Bybit-hybrid verdict-collapse (PF 2.63 → 1.14) is real and
  reproducible on the data we have.** The Coinbase result was the only
  PF-clearing result; the bar-source dependency means we don't know
  whether v1.1 has a real edge on BitUnix-venue execution.
- Architectural v2 (regime classifier) likely needed if v1.1 fails
  on out-of-sample regimes

**What the cutover decision memo must name explicitly (UPDATED 2026-05-18
post-Branch-A addendum):**

The 60-day shadow data must discriminate between two competing
hypotheses, both consistent with the current evidence:

1. **Bar-fidelity-limited hypothesis (H1).** The Bybit-hybrid verdict-
   collapse is an artifact of 3m trade-resolution (vs Coinbase 1m).
   Prediction: shadow PF reverts to ≥1.20 once running on live
   BitUnix bars at native resolution.
2. **Over-fit / cross-venue-price hypothesis (H2).** v1.1 was tuned
   (intentionally or via correctness fixes) to v1.0's Coinbase-1m
   artifacts, OR alert-time effective entry prices differ
   meaningfully between Coinbase and BitUnix-proximate venues.
   Prediction: shadow PF stays near 1.14 in paper.

**Partial in-session resolution (Branch A addendum, 2026-05-18):**
The v3 report's Block C predicted 1m trade-resolution could disambiguate
H1 locally. The Branch A addendum
(`reports/gate_backtest_2026-05-17_v3_bybit_hybrid.md` § Addendum) ran
that experiment using Bitunix native 1m bars (Bybit 1m is geo-blocked
from local network). Result:

| Metric | 3m baseline | 1m Bitunix arm | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profit factor | 1.14 | **1.30** | +0.16 |
| Win rate | 31.2% | **35.5%** | +4.3 pp |
| Total R | +2.61 | +5.24 | +2.63 |
| Fires | 32 | 31 | -1 |

**Interpretation of the addendum:**
- **H1 is real but partial.** Moving from 3m to 1m on the same gate-
  pass alerts recovers ~+0.16 PF and ~+4.3 pp WR. PF crosses the
  1.20 acceptance bar for the first time on Bitunix-proximate data.
  Direction matches H1's prediction; magnitude is modest.
- **H1 alone cannot bridge to v1.1/Coinbase's PF=2.63 / WR=54.8%.**
  A ~1.3 PF gap and ~20pp WR gap remain. This residual is what H2
  (cross-venue alert-time price differs, or over-fit) would need to
  account for. H2 is **not confirmed** — could also be CVD-fallback
  artifacts, gate-input bar-source artifacts, or other factors.
- **The asymmetric resolution effect did NOT occur** on this 17d
  window. SL count fell 18 → 17. The 3m result was not optimistically
  biased by SL-smoothing on this sample. (Out-of-sample windows could
  still show the effect; n=1 window can't establish the distribution.)

**Updated prior for shadow data (REQUIRED in the cutover memo):**

Expected shadow PF range: **[1.14, 2.63]** with central tendency near
the middle. The disambiguation is **not binary** — paper data should
be expected to land in a range reflecting partial-H1-recovery +
partial-residual-effect, not at either extreme. Specifically:

- Shadow PF lands near 2.63 → both H1 (resolution) AND something else
  (live native bars + real CVD vs the proxy + alert-source provenance)
  were each load-bearing in v1.1's apparent Coinbase edge.
- Shadow PF lands near 1.30 → H1 was the dominant local-recoverable
  factor; the residual gap is real v1.1 limitation (over-fit, regime,
  or venue-execution).
- Shadow PF lands near 1.14 → H1's local recovery did not generalize;
  the over-fit interpretation strengthens.
- Shadow PF lands below 1.14 → v1.1 has additional out-of-sample
  weakness not visible in any backtest variant.

The cutover memo's pre-data prior is now the central interval, not the
binary either/or. Reading shadow data on a binary prior would
mis-attribute partial outcomes.

**What this addendum did NOT close:**
- Cross-venue confound on the resolution path (Bitunix 1m vs the
  Bybit 1m we can't access). Cross-venue close alignment is tight
  (median 0.53bps) but not zero.
- Whether v1.1 generalizes to mixed-regime data. The 17d window is
  hostile-regime (29/32 fires were sells). Block B's synth-31d
  already showed regime fragility for the synth alert stream.
- Whether real Bybit CVD (vs the OHLCV-proxy tick-rule fallback used
  100% in both arms) materially shifts the score distribution.

**Decision-process implication:** The addendum strengthens the case
for proceeding to paper cutover (PF clears the bar on the most-
favorable trade-resolution arm we can build locally), but does not
escalate it past the auto_execute_caps harmonization gate or the
positive-EV paper data gate per CLAUDE.md § 1.

### 9c. Regime-classifier layer — architectural v2

**What it would do:** Modulate per-factor weights OR thresholds based
on a regime classification (trend / range / chop) computed at each
evaluation. Possible inputs: ADX, ATR percentile rank, volume profile,
multi-TF EMA alignment quality.

**Why deferred:** v1.1 is a v1 architecture. We don't know yet whether
the regime-conditioning step is necessary; paper data should answer that.

**Trigger to revisit:** Paper data shows v1.1 systematically over-fires
or under-fires in specific regimes the 17d backtest didn't capture.
Public-domain literature suggests this is the natural next architectural
step.

### 9d. **Prod audit-data retention** — operational improvement (HIGH PRIORITY)

**What we discovered 2026-05-17:** Prod's audit_event table starts at
2026-04-30. Whatever process (VM re-provision, schema rebuild, manual
truncate, etc.) discarded prior audit history has destroyed the data
needed for any backtest against pre-2026-04-30 prod-alert history.

**Why this is more significant than a typical backlog item:** This is
**infrastructure work that has compounding cost the longer it's
deferred.** Every additional day of operation generates more audit
data; every time a future incident wipes it, more value is lost.
Today this blocked the truly-independent v1.1 generalization test.
Tomorrow it blocks the next backtest. The fix has linear cost; the
deferral has quadratic cost.

**Concrete asks:**
1. **VM lifecycle should preserve audit data across provisioning
   events.** A VM re-provision should not silently destroy
   `/home/azureuser/trading_corp/data/trading_corp.db`.
2. **Audit data should be replicated to a longer-retention store**
   (Azure Blob, separate Postgres, S3, anything outside the VM
   instance) so future backtest work has access to historical alerts
   even if the VM is rebuilt.
3. **Document the retention policy** — future contributors should
   know what data is preserved across what events and what isn't.

**Trigger to act:** Next significant infrastructure work touching the
VM, OR before the next backtest validation that needs > 60 days of
prod audit history. Whichever comes first.

## 10. What we still don't know

Honest enumeration. This section is for surfacing gaps in our reasoning,
not papering over them.

- **Does v1.1 generalize to non-sell-tape regimes?** Backtest can't
  answer; paper exposure is the test.
- **Is the 31-trade sample size of v1.1's 17d backtest large enough
  for the PF=2.6 / WR=55% claims to hold up?** Statistically, no.
  Confidence intervals are wide. Paper exposure should add ~5-10
  trades per week.
- **Would the May 16 score-engine variants (H2/H6b/H4b) interact
  positively or negatively with the v1.1 gate?** We don't know; not
  tested.
- **Are the 5m volatility factor's thresholds correctly calibrated
  for 3m scalp horizons?** ATR(14) on 5m bars is a 70-min lookback;
